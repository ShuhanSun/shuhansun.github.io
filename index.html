Background

Engineering experience informing AI safety research

I build backend platforms and decision systems that operate under real production constraints. That experience shapes my research perspective: AI safety is not only a model-level problem. Once an agent can call tools, modify data, trigger workflows, or make decisions, safety depends on the full execution path—including instructions, permissions, policies, system state, and downstream effects.

I am pursuing a PhD in Artificial Intelligence and developing research around secure, constraint-aware AI agents and evaluation methods that expose failures before deployment.

Research

Research interests

I am most interested in AI systems that do more than generate text: agents that observe, decide, and act.

01

AI Agent Execution Safety

Methods for determining whether a proposed agent action is safe, authorized, policy-compliant, and appropriate before execution.

02

Prompt Injection & Instruction Integrity

Defenses against untrusted or adversarial instructions entering through webpages, files, messages, retrieved context, or connected tools.

03

Constraint-Aware Decision Orchestration

Decision layers that optimize utility while enforcing safety constraints, permissions, business rules, user preferences, and operational limits.

04

Agent Evaluation & Benchmarks

Reproducible evaluations for attack resistance, unsafe action rates, policy compliance, recovery behavior, and reliability under realistic tool use.

Questions I am exploring

  • Can an agent reliably recognize dangerous execution paths before taking an irreversible action?
  • How should systems separate trusted objectives from untrusted instructions encountered during a workflow?
  • What benchmark design best predicts whether an agent will remain safe when connected to real tools and changing environments?
